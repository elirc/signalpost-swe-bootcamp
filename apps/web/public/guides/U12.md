# U12 — Ownership, mentoring and readiness

**Senior practice · Learning unassessed.** Assemble honest evidence of independent work, contribution and judgment across time.

Prerequisites: U11. Challenge-out requires evidence; see ASSESSMENT.md.

## Learn

Senior responsibilities include selecting valuable work under ambiguity, reducing risk for others and improving a shared system over time. A syllabus alone cannot provide that experience. A good handoff enables another person to run, diagnose and change the work. Mentoring develops the other person’s reasoning; writing their solution removes that evidence. Readiness varies with team and role. Report observed behavior, assistance and practical limits with dated artifacts, then choose the next useful responsibility.

- Connect work to an actual user/team problem and revisit the effect.
- Maintain responsibility across more than one change.
- Attribute personal work, agent output and reviewer feedback separately.
- Do not average away a critical gap with unrelated strong scores.

## Worked example

```text
Claim: independently improved diagnosis.
Evidence: reproduction, corrective PR/regression, runbook used by another person, later drill.
Limit: controlled local exercise, no sustained production support yet.
Next: own a bounded real issue through review and follow-up.
```

The claim matches the artifact and names its limit. A demo without another reader or later follow-up leaves collaboration and retained learning unobserved.

## U12-01 — Audit the portfolio

**PAIR · Learn with guidance · 2–4 focused hours, provisional.**

Select two independent features, two investigations, one design and one review/handoff. Attribute help and remove unsupported claims.

Acceptance:

- Each claim has artifact, date and observed behavior.
- Separate simulation from real users/operations.
- Identify the weakest essential skill and a focused remedy.

Done:

- Acceptance checked against actual output and a learner-owned artifact.
- Help used recorded; learner explains behavior and limitations.
- Feedback and delayed transfer pending until performed. Agent-prepared code earns no learning credit.

Out of scope: Extra frameworks, real notifications, paid infrastructure and unrelated platform work. Use synthetic data and the smallest exercise that proves the skill.

## Delayed transfer

Enter an unfamiliar small repository: run it, map one behavior, fix a bounded issue and hand off reasoning without a tutorial.

Revisit after 2–7 days and around three weeks with the prior solution closed. These are scheduling defaults, not scientific mastery thresholds.

## Debugging scenario

A polished portfolio is mostly agent-authored. Inspect contributions and request independent transfer before claiming competence.

Injected defects belong only in disposable copies with synthetic data and external effects disabled.

## If stuck

Return to the weakest gate and rebuild a small piece. Real responsibility requires participation, not more course generation.

## Readiness

Demonstrate both competencies on your own task and a changed example. Apply ASSESSMENT.md; ratings describe behavior, not job titles.

Keep small-exercise records to three lines: intention; result/artifact; help/next gap. Portfolio work also needs a plan, review and handoff.
